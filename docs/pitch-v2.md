# Saathi pitch v2: a trusted pair of hands at the door, on one phone call

> Mentor feedback we're answering: *"AI that books things is already done (Grok, any agent can do it). What do you understand about seniors that everyone else hasn't figured out? India's informal economy is turning into a gig economy, so a trained person could be at a senior's door within minutes. Look at Pronto and Urban Company. Give us the right idea, one that's feasible and makes money."*

## The one-line pitch

**Saathi is one phone number a senior can call and say "I need someone". An AI on the call understands her in Tamil or English and sends a trained, background-checked Saathi helper from a hub within 2 km, the same helper she already knows, to her door in about 15 minutes. Her children approve the visit, pay for it and watch it happen live.**

AI is the front desk. People do the work. The moat is the trusted local network, not the AI.

---

## 1. What we understand that others haven't

**Insight 1: seniors don't need more information. They need hands.**
Most of what goes wrong for a senior living alone is small, physical and urgent: a fused bulb, a TV that won't turn on, a gas cylinder to change, a prescription to collect, a bank visit, someone to sit with her at the clinic. An AI agent can find a doctor; it can't walk her to one. Among Indian elders who have trouble with daily activities, only about **23% get help from family** (LASI, IIPS). The gap is the last ten metres, not the last click.

**Insight 2: the need is vague, so app menus fail them.**
Urban Company and Pronto sell clear tasks to a 30-year-old with an app: "2BHK cleaning, 60 minutes". A senior says "someone to help me, the tap is leaking and I need to go to the bank." No app menu fits that. A phone call does. The AI turns her vague ask into a clear job and a helper with the right skills.

**Insight 3: for seniors, trust is a person, not a rating.**
Gig apps send a different stranger every time, which is fine for cleaning and frightening for an 80-year-old alone at home (and for her son 1,500 km away). We assign each senior **the same one or two helpers**, who know her house, her tablets and her habits. The relationship is the product.

**Insight 4: the payer and the user are different people.**
Her children pay, usually from another city or another country. They want proof: who came, when, what was done. She wants a voice and a familiar face. Saathi serves both: a phone call for her, a live app for them.

**Insight 5: the supply already exists; it just needs to be organised.**
India's gig workforce is projected to grow from **7.7 million (2020–21) to 23.5 million by 2029–30** (NITI Aayog, 2022). Pronto and Urban Company have shown that hubs of shift workers can reach a home in 10–15 minutes. We use the same model, add elder-specific training, and get a workforce that is cheap enough for families and dignified for the helpers.

### Why now, why India, why Tamil Nadu

- India had about **14 crore people over 60 in 2021 (10.1%)**, heading to **22.7 crore by 2036** (UNFPA–IIPS India Ageing Report 2023).
- Elders living alone or with only a spouse rose from **22% to 27%** between 2011 and 2017–18, driven by children migrating for work. Tamil Nadu has among the highest rates of elders living alone (LASI; Down To Earth).
- 10-minute services have already trained urban India to expect a person at the door quickly. Nobody has built that for the people who need it most.

## 2. Why "an AI that books things" is not the business

| | What it does | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| **Grok, ChatGPT agents, browser agents** | Search, compare, fill forms, book | On a screen. Needs a smartphone, typing and comfort with apps. Can't open a door, lift a cylinder or sit beside her. |
| **Urban Company InstaHelp, Pronto, Snabbit** | A house-help worker in 10–15 minutes | Built for app-using adults and fixed tasks (cleaning, dishes). A different worker each time; no elder training; no phone line for seniors. |
| **Emoha, Samarth, Anvayaa (elder-care companies)** | Care managers, emergency help, community | Premium (Emoha's plans start around **₹3,299/month** and go to about ₹10,000). Scheduled, not 15-minute; heavy on staff. |
| **108 and private ambulances** | Medical emergencies | Only for emergencies. Nobody comes to fix the fan or take her to the bank. |
| **Papa (US): "grandkids on demand"** | Vetted companions for older adults | Proves the model: about **$240M raised at a $1.4B valuation**, paid by US insurers (Medicare Advantage). Not in India; scheduled visits, not on-demand. |
| **Saathi** | Phone call → AI understands → the same trained helper at the door in ~15 min → family approves and watches | Medical care stays with doctors and nurses; we refer to them. |

**Our one sentence for judges:** "Booking is a commodity; any AI can do it. A trusted person at a senior's door in 15 minutes is not, and that is what we're building. The AI just makes it cheap to run."

## 3. How it works

1. **She calls one number** (or Saathi calls her for the daily check-in). It works on any phone, with no app, in Tamil or English.
2. **She says what she needs**, however she says it: "My tablets are over and the bulb in the kitchen is gone."
3. **The AI turns it into a job**: what needs doing, what skills it needs, how urgent it is, and whether it can be done online (a medicine order, a bill) or needs a person.
4. **The family approves it in one tap** (or has set an auto-approve limit, for example up to ₹300). Emergencies never wait for approval.
5. **The nearest available Saathi helper is assigned**, preferring the one she already knows. The family sees their name, photo and ETA.
6. **The helper arrives.** She hears their name from Saathi before they knock, and the helper checks in by saying a visit code she was given on the call.
7. **The work is logged**: before and after photos, the receipt, a two-line note ("Changed kitchen bulb; collected Amlodipine from Apollo, ₹212"). Her children see it live.
8. **Saathi calls her back** that evening: "Did Priya fix the bulb? Are you happy with it?" That rating goes into who gets sent next time.

**In an emergency** (a fall, chest pain), Saathi's existing relay still runs (family alerted, ambulance to the right hospital), and **the nearest helper on shift goes to her door as the first person on the scene**, usually before the ambulance. That joins our original emergency product to the everyday one.

### What helpers do (and don't)

| Tier | Examples | Training |
|---|---|---|
| Errands | Pharmacy and grocery pickup, gas booking and cylinder change, paying a bill at the counter | Basic onboarding |
| Around the house | Bulbs, TV and phone help, setting up a pillbox, a simple meal | Onboarding + elder etiquette |
| Going out with her | Hospital, bank, temple and post office visits; waiting with her | + fall risk, wheelchair, hospital paperwork |
| Company | Sitting and talking, reading the newspaper, a walk | + dementia basics, spotting loneliness and scams |
| **Not us** | Nursing, injections, wound care, personal hygiene (phase 1) | Referred to home-nursing partners |

Helpers never handle her ATM PIN, OTPs or passwords, and never keep cash. Payments go through the family's app.

## 4. Business model (feasible, and it makes money)

### Who pays
- **Children of seniors** (main payer), including NRIs paying from abroad.
- **Later:** employers (a "parents' care" benefit), health insurers and hospitals (after a patient goes home), housing societies with many seniors.

### Plans

| Plan | Price to family | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| **Saathi Call** | ₹499/month | Daily check-in call, errands done online, emergency relay, family app. Helper visits pay as you go. |
| **Saathi Care** | ₹2,499/month | Everything in Call + **8 helper hours a month**, the same helper every time, priority dispatch, a monthly "how she's doing" note. |
| **Pay as you go** | ₹299 per hour (minimum 30 minutes, ₹149) | Any visit, any plan. |

For comparison: Emoha's plans start around ₹3,299/month; a full-time maid in Chennai costs much more and can't do errands outside.

### Unit economics (our estimates; check before quoting)

**One pay-as-you-go hour:**

| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Family pays | 299 |
| Helper earns | 180 (Urban Company pays InstaHelp partners ₹150–180/hr) |
| Insurance, background check, training (spread out) | 25 |
| Payment fees, AI call and support | 20 |
| **Saathi keeps** | **≈ 74 (about 25%)** |

**One Saathi Care senior per month:**

| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Family pays | 2,499 |
| 8 helper hours × ₹180 | 1,440 |
| Daily AI calls (about 90 minutes a month; assumes ~₹4/min for an Indian voice-AI stack at volume) | 360 |
| Insurance, checks, support | 150 |
| **Saathi keeps** | **≈ 550 (about 22%)** |

**What makes it work: density.** One hub of about 8 helpers on shifts can serve about **150–200 seniors within 2 km**. With 100 Care seniors and pay-as-you-go visits, a hub pays for itself (helpers, a small hub room and one coordinator). The AI does the job of a call centre and dispatch desk, which is how we keep the costs below Emoha's.

### Market size (rough; we'll say these are estimates)
- About **14 crore** Indians over 60 today.
- About 27% live alone or with only a spouse: roughly **3.8 crore**.
- In cities, with a child who can pay ₹500–2,500 a month: our estimate is **25–30 lakh households**.
- At ₹1,500 a month on average, that's about **₹5,000 crore a year**, before employers and insurers.

## 5. Go-to-market: how we'd start (and what we can do as students)

**Pilot: Tambaram and Chromepet, Chennai, 12 weeks.**
1. **Demand:** 50 seniors through 3 housing societies, a senior citizens' forum and 2 temples or churches, plus our own grandparents. Families get the first month free.
2. **Supply:** 6 helpers recruited from self-help groups, NSS volunteers and trained caregivers. Police verification, two days of elder-care training from us and a partner NGO, and accident cover.
3. **One hub:** a room in a housing society, shifts from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
4. **What we measure:** time to the door (target under 20 minutes), the share of asks we complete, how often she calls again (the real sign of trust), family NPS, and helper hours used per shift.
5. **Kill rule:** if seniors don't ask again within 4 weeks, or helpers are idle more than half their shift, we change the offer before we grow.

**Then:** more hubs in Chennai, then Coimbatore and Bengaluru, and sell to employers so the parents of employees in other cities are covered.

## 6. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| **Safety and trust** (a helper harms or cheats a senior) | Police verification, ID checks, a visit code she says on the door, live tracking, photo logs, no cash, no PINs, two helpers for some tasks, insurance. Papa in the US faced public safety criticism in 2022; we treat trust as the product, not a feature. |
| **Not enough seniors in one area to keep helpers busy** | Start in dense, senior-heavy societies; give helpers a guaranteed shift wage; fill idle time with scheduled visits and online errands. |
| **Urban Company or Pronto adds a "senior" option** | They're built for tasks; we're built for the relationship (the same helper, the phone line, the family's view, the emergency link). We could also partner with them for extra helpers at peak times. |
| **Medical liability** | We don't give medical advice or care. Saathi refers to doctors, nurses and 108. |
| **Helpers' welfare** (gig work criticism; Urban Company renamed "Insta Maids" to "InstaHelp" after a backlash) | Fair hourly pay, shift guarantees, insurance, training certificates and respectful names ("Saathi helper"). |
| **Cost of the AI voice** | Short calls, Indian voice-AI providers, and the AI only handles calls; dispatch and records are ordinary software. |

## 8. Q&A bank (new questions)

**"Isn't this just Urban Company for old people?"**
Urban Company sells tasks to people who use apps. We sell a relationship to people who don't: the same helper, reached by a phone call, with her family watching. Their workers aren't trained for seniors, and they send someone new each time.

**"What does the AI actually do, if humans do the work?"**
It's the front desk and the memory. It answers in Tamil at 2 a.m., turns "I need someone" into a clear job, knows her tablets and her helper, does online errands itself, calls her back, and spots red flags (chest pain, a scam call). Without it, we'd need a call centre, and the price would look like Emoha's.

**"How is 15 minutes possible?"**
The same way Pronto and InstaHelp do it: helpers wait on shift at a local hub, and we only open in areas with enough seniors. Most asks aren't emergencies, so 15–30 minutes is fine, and we say so honestly.

**"How do you stop a helper from cheating or harming her?"**
Police verification, ID, training, a visit code, live tracking, photo logs, no cash and no PINs, ratings from her phone call, and insurance. And because the same helper visits, a relationship builds; a stranger every time is riskier.

**"Why would helpers choose you over Urban Company?"**
Steadier work (repeat visits and shift guarantees), lighter work than heavy cleaning, a care certificate they can build a career on, and respect.

**"Who pays if her children can't?"**
At first, families who can. Later, employers, insurers, hospitals (after discharge) and government elder-care schemes. This is how Papa grew in the US, through insurers.

**"What did you actually build?"**
The phone call with Saathi (real AI voice, Tamil and English), real-web errands (real Apollo Pharmacy, real TNPDCL bill page, real doctors), the family app with live updates, and the emergency relay with a neighbour responder. Dispatching paid helpers is the business we'd build next. In the demo, the neighbour's phone plays the helper.
